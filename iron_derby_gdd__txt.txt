
KINETIC THEORY GAMES

IRON DERBY
Game Design Document — v0.1


Physics-driven arcade demolition derby

Platform: PC (Steam)
Engine: Unreal Engine 5
Target Launch: Q3 2026
Price Point: €4.99 – €7.99

1. Game Overview
1.1 Concept
Iron Derby is a physics-driven arcade demolition derby where you score points by destroying opponents and surviving. Contained, chaotic, and deeply satisfying to clip. Every collision has weight, every panel that flies off is a moment worth recording.

1.2 Pillars
    • Destruction feels incredible — every hit has weight, sound, and visual feedback
    • Readable chaos — 10 cars crashing at once, but you always know what's happening to you
    • One more round — score-attack loop that is addictive in 5-minute sessions

1.3 Elevator Pitch
Wreckfest's destruction satisfaction in a micro-scope arcade format — no race to win, just survive and destroy. Designed for solo play, streaming moments, and sub-€8 impulse purchases on Steam.

2. Gameplay
2.1 Core Loop
The player enters an arena in a car. A 3-minute timer starts. Score points by ramming opponents — harder hits score more. Survive until the timer ends or be the last car standing. Post-session leaderboard. Play again.

2.2 Scoring System
Scoring is event-driven based on collision impulse magnitude. Every hit is mapped to a point value proportional to the relative velocity at the moment of impact.

Action	Points
Light hit (50k+ impulse)	10 pts
Medium hit (150k+ impulse)	25 pts
Heavy hit (250k+ impulse)	50 pts + floating number
Panel detach (opponent)	+20 pts bonus
Elimination (opponent on fire)	+100 pts
Survival bonus (full 3 min)	+50 pts
Consecutive hits (multiplier)	x1.5 after 3 in a row

2.3 Session Structure
    • Pre-session: car select screen (up to 3 variants at launch)
    • Arena: 3-minute timer, score accumulates in real time
    • Floating world-space score numbers appear on each hit
    • Fire state at 80% damage — countdown to elimination
    • Post-session: score summary, personal best, leaderboard rank
    • Instant restart — no menus between rounds

2.4 Car Variants (v1 Target)

Variant	Mass	Top Speed	Strengths
Bruiser (balanced)	1800 kg	Medium	All-round, forgiving, good for new players
Juggernaut (tank)	2400 kg	Slow	Massive impact force, survives longer
Speedster (agile)	1200 kg	Fast	Hard to catch, high ram speed, low HP


3. Destruction System
3.1 Three-Layer Approach
Destruction is implemented in three compounding layers, each adding a dimension of feedback and physicality.

Layer 1 — Cosmetic (all hits above minimum threshold)
    • Impact decals stick to car surface (dirt, scratches, scorch)
    • Directional spark burst at collision point (Niagara GPU, 0.3s lifetime)
    • Triggered via OnComponentHit, zero physics cost

Layer 2 — Structural (medium hits)
    • Morph targets on car body mesh blend toward pre-authored crumple shapes
    • 4 morph targets per car: front, rear, left side, right side
    • Damage float (0-1) per zone drives morph weight in real time
    • Affects visual feel without expensive runtime mesh deformation

Layer 3 — Part Detachment (heavy hits)
    • Geometry Collections activate and simulate as rigid bodies
    • Panels fly off with impulse matching the collision direction
    • Detached panels persist as arena floor obstacles for 10 seconds
    • Adds emergent chaos — debris becomes a hazard

3.2 Handling Degradation
Damage is not purely cosmetic — it affects how the car drives. This creates a natural comeback tension and makes survival feel earned.

Damage Zone	Effect on Handling
Front heavy damage	Steering reduced, understeer increases
Engine (hood)	Max RPM drops, acceleration suffers
Rear heavy damage	Oversteer increases, traction drops
Wheel hit	That wheel loses grip, car pulls
Fire state (80%+ damage)	Speed capped at 60% of maximum

Handling degradation is capped at 40% reduction from baseline — impaired but never undrivable.

3.3 Fire State
    • Triggered at 80% total damage accumulation
    • Point light + particle effect + damage-over-time tick
    • Natural elimination countdown — readable from across the arena
    • Opponent elimination awards 100 bonus points to the player who dealt most damage


4. Arena Design
4.1 v1 — The Bowl
A flat circular dirt arena with angled outer walls. Simple, readable geometry that encourages head-on confrontations. No escape routes — high density of action at all times.
    • Diameter: approximately 60m
    • Outer walls: angled at 30 degrees to deflect cars back into the arena
    • Surface: dirt — visible tyre tracks accumulate during session
    • Central obstacle cluster: 3-5 indestructible concrete pillars forcing path variation

4.2 Arena Hazards (Month 2)
    • Collapsing wall segments — heavy impact can break a section open, but the gap is dangerous
    • Fire zones — patches of burning ground deal passive fire damage on contact
    • Debris from destroyed panels accumulates — dynamic obstacle layer

4.3 v2 Arena — The Figure-8 (Month 3)
A figure-8 track layout with a central crossover zone. Higher speed, longer range rams, and a crossover collision point that generates natural highlight moments.


5. Technical Overview
5.1 Engine & Physics
    • Unreal Engine 5 — Chaos Vehicle Movement Component as base
    • Chaos Physics for rigid body simulation and Geometry Collections
    • Arcade tuning: high grip, snappy steering, disabled ABS/traction sim
    • Car mass: 1200–2400 kg depending on variant (critical for impact feel)

5.2 Performance Budget
Performance constraints are set early and enforced throughout development to prevent late-stage optimization crises.

Constraint	Limit
Active Geometry Collection chunks	200 max simultaneous
Detached panels alive in arena	40 (pool and recycle)
Destruction LOD distance	Full detail within 15m, simplified beyond
Niagara spark particles per emitter	50 cap
Target frame rate	60 fps on mid-range hardware (GTX 1070 equiv.)

5.3 Camera
    • Third-person follow cam with slight lag and FOV boost at high speed
    • Screen shake on impact — amplitude scales with collision impulse strength
    • Collision camera shake is the single highest-ROI feel improvement


6. Development Plan
6.1 Milestone Overview

Milestone	Goal
Month 1 — Vertical Slice	Fun core loop: 1 arena, 1 car, basic destruction, session timer, local leaderboard
Month 2 — Alpha	3 car variants, basic AI opponents, arena hazards, Steam page live
Month 3 — Beta / Launch	2 arenas, full polish pass, trailer, Steam Next Fest (if timing), launch

6.2 Month 1 Priority Tasks
Month 1 is intentionally minimal. The only success criterion is: play it for 20 minutes without getting bored.
    • Unreal project setup + Chaos Vehicle base
    • Car controller feel tuning (timeboxed to 3 days)
    • Collision impulse mapped to score event
    • Layer 1: Impact VFX and decals
    • Layer 2: Morph target denting (front / rear / sides)
    • Layer 3: Geometry Collection panel detachment (front bumper only)
    • Arena blockout — flat bowl geometry
    • Session timer (3 min) + score counter
    • Camera shake on collision
    • Basic engine and collision SFX
    • Fire state (80% damage threshold)
    • Performance budget pass (stat chaos profiling)

6.3 Scope Cut Rules
The following are explicitly out of scope for v1 and must not be added during Month 1:
    • Multiplayer of any kind
    • More than 1 arena
    • More than 1 car
    • Main menu or settings screen
    • AI opponents
    • Music
Everything above is a Month 2 or Month 3 concern.


7. Commercial Strategy
7.1 Market Context
The demolition derby / vehicular destruction genre has a healthy PC audience. Wreckfest proved the high-end appetite (1M+ copies). The indie gap is an arcade-tone, low-price entry point that does not try to compete as a simulation.
KEO (Steam Early Access, vehicular combat with weapons and online multiplayer) is a surface-level competitor but targets a fundamentally different game loop — weapons-based team PvP vs. physics-based solo destruction. KEO's struggle with player counts validates the decision to avoid multiplayer dependency.

7.2 Price & Positioning
    • Launch price: €4.99 – €7.99 (impulse purchase territory)
    • No DLC planned for v1 — complete experience at launch
    • Potential post-launch: additional arena pack at €1.99

7.3 Marketing Timeline

TikTok / YouTube Shorts — destruction clips	Weekly from Week 3
Steam page live + wishlist open	Start of Month 2
Steam Next Fest submission (if timing aligns)	Month 3

Launch at €4.99 – €7.99	End of Month 3

7.4 Content Strategy
Destruction games clip themselves. Every session generates shareable moments — pile-ups, panel fly-offs, last-second survival. The content strategy is built on this:
    • Raw 30-second destruction clips — no narration, no editing
    • Before/after car damage state comparisons
    • Development build progress devlogs on YouTube
    • Physics experiments and behind-the-scenes tuning clips

7.5 Competitive Comparison

Game	Price	Scope	Differentiator vs Iron Derby
Wreckfest	€29.99	Full	Sim-adjacent, race-focused, full production
Burnout Paradise Remaster	€19.99	Full	Older, racing-primary, no active dev
Derby: Extreme Racing	€3.99	Micro	Low visual quality, no physics depth
KEO	€9.99	EA	Multiplayer-dependent, weapons-based
Iron Derby (target)	€4.99–7.99	Micro	Arcade destruction, physics depth, solo viable


8. Risk Register

Risk	Severity	Likelihood	Mitigation

Chaos physics performance in large collisions	High	Medium	Cap simultaneous chunks, enforce LOD destruction early
Game feels empty without good AI	Medium	High	Populate arena with dummies early, add environmental chaos
3-month timeline slips	Medium	Medium	Cut to 1 arena, 1 car, score-attack only — still shippable
Low wishlist traction on Steam	Medium	Low	Post destruction clips weekly from Week 2 to build audience

9. Reference & Inspiration
9.1 Feel References
    • Wreckfest — destruction model, damage feel, weight of impacts
    • Burnout 3: Takedown — camera work, impact feedback, crash cam energy
    • Carmageddon — destructive freedom, dark humour, vehicular chaos

9.2 Visual References
    • Hotshot Racing — low-poly arcade aesthetic, strong colour contrast
    • Wreckfest — chunky, grounded vehicle design language
Target style: stylized but weighty. Chunky low-poly cars with physically convincing impacts. Lower art budget, strong visual identity, GIFs well.

9.3 Anti-References (what we are NOT building)
    • Not a racing game — there is no race to win
    • Not a simulation — arcade feel, not physics realism
    • Not multiplayer-dependent — solo score-attack is the primary mode
    • Not feature-complete at launch — contained, polished, focused
